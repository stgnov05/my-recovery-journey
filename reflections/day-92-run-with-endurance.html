Run With Endurance

A daily reflection by Steven Gregory — in recovery since August 20, 2026.

I love that this verse doesn’t tell me to run faster. It tells me to run with endurance — and first, to strip off every weight that slows me down.

For me, the weights have names: fear about what comes next, shame about what came before, resentment I keep picking back up. None of them help me run. They just make the same road heavier. And the sin that “so easily trips us up” — for me, that’s the drink, waiting at the edge of every hard day to offer itself as a shortcut. There are no shortcuts on this road. There is only the next step, taken sober.

And I’m not running alone. That “huge crowd of witnesses” — I think about the people in the rooms, the ones who ran this race before me and are still cheering from the sidelines, and the ones running beside me now. I think about you, reading this. We’re witnesses for each other. Your one day encourages my one day, and mine encourages yours.

The race God set before me isn’t a race against anyone else. It’s the road in front of me, run at the pace of one day at a time, with my eyes on Jesus — the One who already ran His race all the way to the end, and who walks this one with me.

“Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a huge crowd of witnesses to the life of faith, let us strip off every weight that slows us down, especially the sin that so easily trips us up. And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us.”— Hebrews 12:1 (NLT)

Consider: What weight are you still carrying into today — and what would it feel like to set it down before your next step?

— Steven
